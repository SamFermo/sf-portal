Alcohol Service Standards

House policy · Effective October 17, 2026 · Revised October 20, 2026

These are San Fermo's standing rules for alcohol service. Several of them are stricter than Washington law on purpose: the Liquor and Cannabis Board encourages licensees to set house policy above the legal minimum, and we do. They apply on every shift. New hires read them in the employee handbook and sign them here; everyone reviews them at the annual Responsible Liquor Sales class.

Permits

  1. Anyone who serves, pours or supervises alcohol holds a current MAST permit (Class 12; Class 13 for staff aged 18 to 20, who may take orders but not pour or mix). Keep a copy uploaded in the portal under Compliance so it is available for inspection. Renewing before it expires is on you; an expired permit takes you off alcohol service until it is renewed. Renew through a state-approved provider: WSLCB MAST page · find an approved class. Kitchen roles do not need MAST.

Checking ID

  1. If they look under 50, card them. That includes regulars and people you know. When in doubt, card. Carding your whole section is always fine.
  2. Acceptable ID is current, unexpired, and has a photo and date of birth: a U.S. or Canadian driver's license or state ID, a passport or passport card, a U.S. military ID, a permanent resident card, a NEXUS or Global Entry card, or a Washington tribal enrollment card. Expired documents, foreign driver's licenses, school IDs and photos of an ID on a phone are not accepted. A vertical Washington license means under 21. Read the date of birth; do not estimate.
  3. At the bar, two checks every time. The manager on duty cards before the guest sits, and the bartender checks again before the first pour.
  4. A bottle of wine means everyone at the table shows ID. No exceptions.

Bottles, tables and sharing

  1. Parents with small children need nothing beyond the ordinary rules. The table to watch is any one you suspect might share alcohol behind your back, a group of young adults with one guest under 21 being the clearest example.
  2. At a table like that, the server pours. The bottle stays at the station, not on the table, and is poured on request only. Tell the rest of the floor about the table so everyone has eyes on it.
  3. Sharing ends the visit. Any table that passes a drink to someone who was not served it is asked to leave. Clear every drink, settle or comp the check as needed, and do not debate it.

Intoxication and pacing

  1. Any sign of intoxication means no more alcohol. No exceptions, no negotiating. Get the manager on duty if you need backup.
  2. Pace rounds conservatively so that cutting someone off is rarely necessary.

Complimentary drinks

  1. An occasional drink on the house is fine for an anniversary or to make up for a service problem, for a guest who is of age and has shown ID. It is never advertised, posted or offered as a standing program.

Non-alcoholic drinks and unfinished wine

  1. Non-alcoholic items live in their own section of the menu and are rung on their own buttons, never as a modified cocktail. Beer under 0.5% ABV is served to adults only.
  2. A guest may take home an unfinished bottle of wine bought with a meal. Recork it and send it in its original bottle.

Staff consumption

This applies to everyone, kitchen included. No employee drinks alcohol or uses drugs during a shift. A shift drink is served only after you have clocked out. Violation is grounds for immediate termination.

Talking with guests

If a guest objects to being carded, apologize and keep it simple: "San Fermo follows Washington Liquor Board requirements and our own house policy to protect our license. Sorry for the hassle."

Do not discuss enforcement activity, inspections or violations with guests.

Questions go to Sam, Sully or the manager on duty. Our LCB enforcement contact is listed in Front Desk.
